What the model sees — entities, not pixels

Hexo is played on an unbounded board, so there is no canvas to convolve over and no fixed input vector to fill in. MantisNet reads a position as a small graph of typed entities — the stones, the live windows, four position-level latents, and the legal cells — connected by relations drawn from frozen, symmetry-folded vocabularies. Everything on this page is drawn from one real position of a real game against the served model, rebuilt by the production input builder.

01the game, in five sentences

Hexo is a two-player placement game on an infinite hexagonal board in axial coordinates (q, r). Player 0 opens with a single stone at the origin; thereafter each player places two stones per turn. A placement must be an empty cell within 8 hex steps of some occupied cell. Six or more of a player’s stones consecutive along one of the board’s three axes win, checked after every placement — a turn can end on its first stone. There are no draws, passes, or captures, and stones are permanent.

02the four entity sets

The featured position below is eleven stones into a real showcase game — Mantis Shrimp v2 (blue, player 0) is about to place the first stone of its third full turn. The builder turns it into:

  • 11 stones — one node each. The only per-stone feature is ownership relative to the side to move (own / opponent). A stone’s coordinates never enter its features; geometry acts only through the typed relations below.
  • 154 live windows — a window is any six-cell line segment currently holding at least one stone, of either player or both. Windows are the tactical vocabulary of the game: every possible win is a window, and every threat is a window filling up.
  • 4 state latents — position-level rows with no board location. They initialize from a learned base plus an embedding of moves_remaining (1 or 2 stones left this turn — the only whole-position scalar the model consumes) and carry global context through the trunk.
  • 420 legal cells — every empty cell within 8 steps of a stone, in the engine’s canonical order. 152 of them are covered; the other 268 lie beyond every live window and start from generic feature embeddings instead.

03window patterns — a 377-word vocabulary

A window’s state is its six slots, each empty, own, or opponent — a ternary string, read mover-relatively (own = the side to move). Reading the string backwards describes the same physical window from the other end, so patterns are folded under reversal: of the 728 nonempty length-6 ternary strings, 351 mirror pairs and 26 palindromes leave a vocabulary of 377 canonical patterns. Each live window enters the network as the learned embedding of its pattern — the pattern is the window’s initial state, and own-only / opponent-only / contested status is a pure function of it, so no separate status feature exists.

04typed relations — how entities connect

Entities alone say nothing about where things stand. All geometry enters through relation types, and every vocabulary is folded under the board’s twelve symmetries, so a position and its rotation, reflection, or colour swap produce identical inputs:

  • Stone ↔ window: 1,458 occupancy classes. When a stone talks to a window that contains it, the message is typed by the joint of the window’s whole pattern and which slot the stone occupies — folded under simultaneous reversal of both. A stone lies in up to 18 windows (six per axis).
  • Cell ↔ window: 726 decoder classes. The same joint typing for a window’s empty slots: the pattern plus which vacancy the cell is. These classes type both the trunk’s cell reads and the output heads’ incidence terms.
  • Stone → cell: 192 radius classes. Every legal cell reads the stones within hex radius 8 through edges typed by three invariant classifications multiplied together: the offset vector’s orbit-48 class (fig 3), own / opponent, and whether the pair shares a board axis — 48 × 2 × 2.
  • Action rows: 729 post-placement classes. Every legal move is also encoded by its consequences: the 18 windows the placed stone would occupy, each typed by the joint of its post-placement pattern and the placement slot (fig 5).
The joint tables are one enumeration: the 729 patterns × 6 slots give 2,187 reversal orbits, of which the empty pattern’s three belong to no live window — the remaining 2,184 split exactly into the 726 decoder classes (empty slots) and 1,458 occupancy classes (occupied slots). These identities are asserted in the builder at import time.

05what is deliberately absent

No absolute coordinates. No axis identity as a feature. No move history, no ply number, no hand-crafted tactical scalars. Everything the model knows arrives through the entities and typed relations above — and every one of them is invariant under the board’s twelve symmetries. The consequence is structural: the network cannot represent the difference between a position and its rotation, reflection, or colour swap, so it computes identical outputs for all of them (up to the engine’s reordering of the legal list). A replay test transforms whole game histories through the engine and demands the permuted outputs back.

This is a different route to symmetry than weight-tied equivariant architectures: there, constrained weights make rotated inputs produce rotated outputs; here, rotated inputs are the same inputs. Both spend zero training on augmentation.
