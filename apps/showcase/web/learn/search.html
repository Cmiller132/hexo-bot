Search & training — one operator, two uses

The critic applies new weights to the policy of the network, and the result is a better policy. This one operation is the core of the system. In play, the operation is a short lookahead: Gumbel sequential halving across 16 candidate moves. In training, the operation is the closed-form KLENT operator, and the network learns from its own improvement. The two halves of this page use real captured data from the served model.

01the effect of the search

On the featured position, the top move of the raw policy is (0, 1) at 27%. The search examined that line, measured a value near 0, and cut it. The cell (−2, 6) was the second choice of the prior at 7%. Its line showed more value at each depth. Its final value was +0.66, and the search selected it. Four turns later, the game ended with a six-in-a-row through that cell.

02the mechanics

The search is Gumbel sequential halving over deterministic lines, not a branching tree. One move decision has these steps:

  1. Root read. One forward of the network gives the prior across all legal cells. The root read is not part of the simulation budget.
  2. Candidates. The top m = 16 cells by ln π + Gumbel noise become candidate lines. On this site, the noise scale is zero. The candidates are the top 16 of the prior, and each game with the same moves is reproducible.
  3. Deepening waves. In each wave, each line that continues extends by one ply. The network evaluates the current position of the line, and the line steps to the most probable reply in that evaluation. Each line is a principal variation. The value of a line is the newest v̂, in the frame of the root player. One batched forward evaluates all positions of a wave.
  4. Halving. After each round of waves, the lines sort by value, and the lower half is cut. The budget divides equally across the rounds. Later rounds examine half the lines at two times the depth (fig 1).
  5. Decision. From the final survivors, the search plays the move with the maximum of
    gumbel + ln π(a) + (50 + max_visits) · value(a)
    The value weight increases with the depth. At the end of the search, the measured values control the result more than the prior.

04training: KLENT self-play

The served checkpoint is iteration 402 of a KLENT self-play run. Each iteration plays 4,096 complete games across 1,024 parallel environments, fits the network for one epoch on that buffer, and then discards the buffer. The training is fully on-policy, without a replay window. At each acting ply, the improved policy comes from the two heads in closed form:

π′(a)  ∝  exp[ (Q̃(a) + τ·ln π(a)) / (τ + λ) ]

τ holds π′ near the policy. λ is the KL limit for one improvement step. Q̃ is the mass-normalized score of the critic (the heads section). The run trained with τ = 0.1 and λ = 0.01. The checkpoint export contains these values, because π′ has no meaning under different parameters. Use the two controls:

the loss targets

Each acting ply writes one training row. Two losses fit the row:

  • Policy cross-entropy against π′. The next prior of the network moves to the improvement of this ply.
  • Critic cross-entropy on the taken action. The target return G is a λ-return over v̂ (λret = 0.939, γ = 0.99). The three-part target is (max(G,0), max(−G,0), 1−|G|). A return of +0.6 teaches 60% win mass and 40% zero mass. Hexo has two placements in each turn, and the perspective changes only at a turn handover. The return chain has the sign +1 in a turn and −1 across a handover (fig 4).

The self-play trains nothing else. The state-value head stays untouched (the v̂ note). There is no Dirichlet noise, there are no forced playouts, and there is no symmetry augmentation. An evaluation harness plays each 25th checkpoint against fixed opponents. The harness measures only, and it does not gate the training. A pooled evaluation across the late checkpoints selected iteration 402.

the changes from the paper

KLENT comes from “Revisiting Regularized Policy Optimization for Stable and Efficient Reinforcement Learning in Two-Player Games” (Ota, Osa, Omura, and Harada; ICML 2026). The paper names the entropy coefficient α and the reverse-KL coefficient β. This site and the codebase name them λ and τ. The run keeps the update rule of the paper and changes the parts in this table. Measured comparison runs on Hexo selected the changed coefficients.

The placement-level MDP is the structural change. A Hexo turn after the opening has two placements, and a win can end either one. Because of this, the return chain steps by placements, with the signs of fig 4. The per-placement mix 0.939 gives 0.939² ≈ 0.882 across a full turn. That equals the per-action value of the paper, so the return horizon in turns does not change. The discount γ = 0.99 adds a small preference for the fast win.

The categorical critic replaces the scalar critic of the paper because acting needs the committed mass M (the heads section). The score Q̃ divides Q by the largest M of the position. A position where the critic expects mostly zero returns keeps a usable score scale. The other changes hold the loop strictly on-policy: one epoch on each corpus, no replay window, and whole-game drops at the ply cap.

05how this site serves it

The bot operates this stack on an Intel Arc A310 (4 GB), through the PyTorch XPU backend in eager mode. One batched forward evaluates the leaf positions of each wave. A numeric parity self-check operates at each boot, before the worker accepts games. The sims control sets the search budget of fig 1: 16, 32, 64, or 128 simulations for each stone. The opening noise is off on the full site. The bot is deterministic: the same moves give the same game.

During play, the live viewer draws the frames of fig 2. The full-board fill is the policy prior, and the rings are the 16 candidates. The arc of a ring fills with the budget share of its line. The centre tick shows the current value of the line. The brightness of a cell follows its live rank. A candidate that becomes dim was cut in a halving round.
